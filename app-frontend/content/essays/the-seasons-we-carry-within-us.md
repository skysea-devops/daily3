## The Autumn Outside My Window

It is a beautiful, sunny Sunday morning in October. Looking out of my window, I can see that autumn has arrived. The trees that greeted me with their bright green leaves just a few weeks ago have begun to turn yellow, orange, and brown. Some have already lost much of their foliage. Others are still holding on to the last traces of summer.

It is a familiar sight, one that returns every year. Yet something about it makes me stop and look a little longer.

![Autumn trees in a park, viewed through the author's apartment window.](/essays/the-seasons-we-carry-within-us/autumn-outside-my-window.jpg "Autumn outside my window, October 2026. Photograph by the author, digitally edited.")

Strangely, the view brings me both peace and sadness. There is something calming about the quiet transformation of the trees, but also something melancholy about watching the leaves fall. Perhaps it is the reminder that even the most familiar things cannot remain unchanged.

Only a few weeks ago, the same trees looked full of life. Nothing extraordinary had happened to them. Autumn had simply arrived, as it always does.

We accept these changes in nature without much resistance. We know that autumn will follow summer, that winter will eventually come, and that spring will bring new life. The trees do not seem troubled by the seasons. They change with them.

Our own lives are different.

We experience beginnings and endings, moments of happiness and periods of sadness. We build relationships, settle into familiar places, develop routines, and gradually become accustomed to the lives we have created. Then something changes, sometimes by choice and sometimes without warning.

Unlike the trees outside my window, we rarely know when our next season will arrive.

And I wonder: Are the seasons of human life as clearly defined as those of nature?

## When Our Own Autumn Arrives

Autumn does not always arrive with falling leaves. In our lives, it can take the form of sorrow, disappointment, unexpected difficulties, or changes that disturb the world we have grown accustomed to.

Sometimes it is the loss of someone we love, an unwanted separation, or a failure we did not expect. Other times, it is leaving a city we have called home for years or having to walk away from a career we once imagined would last a lifetime.

![A woman in black walking alone down an autumn park path strewn with fallen leaves, under a grey sky.](/essays/the-seasons-we-carry-within-us/autumn-day-sokolniki.jpg "Autumn Day. Sokolniki, Isaac Levitan, 1879 — State Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow. Public Domain.")

What these experiences share is the way they change something familiar about our lives, often leaving us uncertain about what comes next.

Leaving a city, for example, means more than changing an address. We leave behind familiar streets, friendships, routines, and memories. Similarly, losing a job can affect more than our financial security. It may change our sense of purpose, our identity, and the future we had imagined for ourselves.

We often define ourselves through the lives we have built, the roles we occupy, and the things we have achieved. When these change or disappear, we may find ourselves questioning not only what we have lost, but also who we are without them.

## The Winters We Live Through

I experienced something like this when I had to leave my military career.

Being a soldier had given me more than a profession. It had provided a certain identity, a status, goals, and a philosophy of life. There was a structure to my days and a direction I had grown accustomed to following.

When that chapter ended, much of what had defined my life seemed to disappear with it.

There was an opportunity to build a different life, certainly. But an opportunity is not the same as a clear path forward. The future remained uncertain, and creating a new beginning required effort without any guarantee of where that effort would lead.

Looking back, I think of that period as a kind of winter.

![Snow-covered village and returning hunters in Pieter Bruegel's winter landscape.](/essays/the-seasons-we-carry-within-us/hunters-in-the-snow.jpg "Hunters in the Snow, Pieter Bruegel the Elder, 1565 — Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna. Public Domain.")

Winter has its own silence. Trees stand bare, the days grow shorter, and much of the activity we associate with the warmer months disappears from view. Yet beneath the apparent stillness, life continues.

There are periods in human life that feel much the same.

Losing a job, going through a separation, or leaving behind a familiar environment can bring a sense of cold uncertainty. We may not know where we belong anymore, what direction to take, or whether the plans we are making will succeed.

The most difficult part is sometimes not the loss itself, but the uncertainty that follows it.

We are accustomed to imagining our lives moving forward. We make plans, set goals, and expect tomorrow to bear some resemblance to today. When that expectation is broken, even ordinary decisions can become difficult.

Of course, people respond to loss differently. Some are able to continue their daily lives while carrying considerable sadness. Others need time before they can begin to imagine a different future. There is no fixed length for such a winter, and no single way to experience it.

Nor should every painful experience be treated as a necessary step toward personal growth. Some losses remain painful. Some changes leave wounds that time does not entirely heal.

Still, life may continue even before the sadness has disappeared.

Sometimes we take small steps without knowing where they will lead. We look for new possibilities, learn unfamiliar skills, adjust our expectations, or simply try to establish a new routine.

We may not recognize these efforts as the beginning of anything significant.

But perhaps the first signs of spring are already there.

## The Quiet Return of Spring

Spring, like winter, often arrives quietly.

We do not wake up one morning to discover that everything has changed. The first signs may be almost unnoticeable: a new interest, a different way of working, someone new entering our life, or a small habit that gradually becomes part of our mornings.

![Flowering fruit trees in a spring landscape painted by Claude Monet.](/essays/the-seasons-we-carry-within-us/spring-fruit-trees-in-bloom.jpg "Spring (Fruit Trees in Bloom), Claude Monet, 1873 — The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Public Domain (CC0).")

None of these things, taken separately, necessarily represents a turning point. Together, however, they can begin to form a different way of living.

A new life rarely arrives fully formed. We build it through decisions, experiments, mistakes, and repeated attempts to find our place.

The Roman emperor and Stoic philosopher Marcus Aurelius reflected on the place of change in the natural world. In his Meditations, he wrote:

> For everything that exists is in a manner the seed of that which will be.
>
> — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Book IV, 36. Translated by George Long, 1862.

The image of a seed is worth considering.

What comes next does not emerge from nothing. Even when a period of our life has ended, something of it remains in the person we have become.

The knowledge we gained, the mistakes we made, the people we encountered, and the difficulties we faced can all influence the choices we make afterward.

This does not mean that every loss was necessary or that suffering must have a purpose. A new beginning does not justify the pain that preceded it.

Spring does not justify winter. It simply follows it.

Nor does a new beginning mean returning to the person we were before. Someone who finds another job after losing a career may discover different ambitions and a different understanding of work. Someone who moves to another city may create new friendships without losing affection for the place they left behind.

Even returning to an old home cannot always restore the life we once had there. Places may remain familiar, but time has passed, and we have changed.

This is perhaps one of the more difficult things to accept about beginning again: we cannot always recover what we have lost, but we may still find ways to live meaningfully with what remains.

Over time, unfamiliar routines become familiar. The effort of starting again becomes part of ordinary life. What once seemed uncertain gradually begins to feel like a place where we belong.

And one day, almost without noticing, we may find ourselves living in a new season.

## The Summers We Find Along the Way

Summer brings a different feeling.

The trees are full again. Days are longer, the landscape is filled with colour, and life seems to open up in ways that were difficult to imagine during winter.

There are summers in our lives, too.

A new routine begins to feel natural. We discover new interests, meet people who bring different perspectives into our lives, and sometimes find ourselves following paths we had never considered before.

There is a particular satisfaction in realizing that we have built something new. Not necessarily something better than what came before, but something different, with its own possibilities and pleasures.

Life can surprise us. An unexpected opportunity may lead to meaningful work, a conversation may become a lasting friendship, and ordinary days gradually fill with new memories.

Yet these experiences do not require us to forget the old ones.

Building a different life has not erased my affection for the one I left behind. I live differently now, and the experiences I have gained have broadened the way I see the world. But I can still remember my former life with warmth, sometimes with nostalgia, and appreciate what those years meant to me.

The Lebanese writer Kahlil Gibran reflected on the relationship between happiness and sadness in The Prophet:

> Your joy is your sorrow unmasked.
>
> — Kahlil Gibran, The Prophet, “On Joy and Sorrow,” 1923.

Perhaps joy and sorrow are not always opposites. An old friendship can bring a smile even when we miss the days we shared. A place we once called home may remain dear to us long after we have found another.

We do not have to choose between appreciating the past and enjoying the present.

A new summer does not erase the seasons that came before it. It simply adds new experiences, relationships, and memories to the life we carry within us.

## All the Seasons Within Us

Looking again at the trees outside my window, I see the familiar signs of autumn. The leaves are changing colour, some are beginning to fall, and the landscape is slowly preparing for winter.

Before long, the branches will be bare. Then spring will arrive, followed by another summer. The same trees will take on different colours and shapes, each season revealing something new.

Our lives move through seasons, too, though rarely according to a predictable calendar.

We may experience an unexpected beginning at forty, discover a new passion at sixty, or find ourselves reconsidering everything we thought we knew at twenty. Some changes come through our own decisions; others arrive without asking our permission.

Each season leaves something with us. Not only memories, but new ways of looking at ourselves and the world.

After living through different chapters of life, we may come to realize that who we are is not limited to a profession, a place, or a particular period of our past. We can become different people without denying who we once were.

Our experiences may also change how we see others. Having known uncertainty ourselves, we might better understand someone struggling to find their way. Having experienced the excitement of a new beginning, we may more easily recognize that same joy in another person.

None of this means that every difficult experience makes us wiser or stronger. But the more seasons we live through, the more possibilities we may discover for understanding life beyond our own familiar circumstances.

Perhaps that is why a single lifetime can feel like so many different lives. We change, we begin again, we form new relationships, and we discover interests and possibilities we could never have imagined before.

Yet through all these changes, we carry something of our past with us. The moments that made us happy, the difficulties we faced, the people and places we left behind. Each becomes part of who we are today, just as a tree continues to grow through the changing seasons.

The trees outside my window are passing through another autumn. And I find myself thinking of the autumns I have left behind, the winters I have lived through, and the springs that brought new beginnings.

Perhaps it is a question worth asking ourselves:

How many winters and springs do we carry within us?
